A warning. A call to return to God.

Zephaniah announces God’s judgment, including judgment on Judah and Jerusalem. This prophetic text warns us of the seriousness of sin; it does not give us a date for the end of the world.

Time is short.
Repent. Return to Jesus.

God will judge the world through His Son. Do not mistake His patience for indifference. Do not hide behind religion. Today, He calls you to leave the darkness and live in His light.

Respond to His callRead the biblical warnings

01 / Judgment is coming

God will not remain silent.

His patience calls us to return to Him. It does not mean He approves of our lives.

God has appointed a day when He will judge the world in righteousness through Jesus Christ, whom He raised from the dead. He now commands everyone, everywhere, to repent.

Acts 17:30–31

Zephaniah warns those who live as though God will never act:

Being “settled on their lees” pictures a settled complacency: living in indifference, convinced that God will never call us to account.

Time is short. Do not delay your response to God. Jesus calls us to watch, but gives us no permission to announce a date for His return: no one knows the day or the hour.

1 Corinthians 7:29 · Matthew 24:36–44 · 2 Peter 3:9–10

02 / False security

Religion cannot hide our lives from God.

Going to church, knowing the Bible, speaking about Jesus or calling ourselves Christians cannot take the place of obedience. We can profess faith while denying it by our actions.

Jesus warns those who call Him “Lord” while practising iniquity. This warning concerns us too. We must examine our own lives.

Matthew 7:21–23 · Luke 6:46 · 1 John 1:6

Jesus continues: the servant does not remain in the house for ever; the Son does. Freedom is found in Him. If the Son makes us free, we are free indeed.

John 8:35–36

03 / The grace of God

Grace teaches us to turn away from sin.

“God is love, so I can keep sinning without repentance.” That conclusion contradicts the apostles’ teaching. Forgiveness does not make evil acceptable.

Jude warns against turning God’s grace into licence for immorality. Paul rejects the idea that we should continue in sin so that grace may abound.

Jude 4 · Romans 6:1–2, 12–14

Salvation is God’s gift, received through faith. We cannot earn it by our works. Yet we are created in Christ Jesus to walk in good works, not to defend our darkness.

Ephesians 2:8–10

A warning to take seriously

Do not despise the Son of God.

This passage solemnly warns against wilful sin and contempt for Jesus’ sacrifice after receiving the knowledge of the truth. Do not use the cross as an excuse to persist in what Jesus came to deliver us from.

This warning does not mean that a fall makes returning to God impossible. Those who acknowledge and confess their sin and return to Jesus have an advocate in Him. Do not justify your sin. Do not despair of His mercy.

1 John 1:8–9; 2:1–2 · Proverbs 28:13

04 / Bring your life into the light

What must we turn away from?

Repentance reaches our daily choices, our hidden habits, and the things we love.

Sexual immorality

Turn away from adultery, sexual relations outside marriage, and pornography. Jesus also exposes the look that deliberately feeds lust.

Matthew 5:27–28 · Hebrews 13:4 · 1 Thessalonians 4:3–5

In the biblical understanding expressed on this site, sexual relations between people of the same sex are also among the practices these texts call us to leave. This concerns conduct; it never authorises contempt or humiliation towards anyone.

Romans 1:26–27 · 1 Corinthians 6:9–11

Greed and materialism

Making money, possessions or success the master of our lives. Scripture calls covetousness idolatry. We cannot serve both God and money.

Colossians 3:5 · Matthew 6:19–24 · 1 Timothy 6:9–10, 17–19

Lies, hatred and injustice

Deceiving, stealing, exploiting, slandering, nurturing hatred or refusing to forgive. Holiness also concerns how we treat other people.

Ephesians 4:25–32 · 1 John 3:15–18 · James 5:1–6

Ungodliness and the works of the flesh

Living without regard for God, feeding jealousy, division, drunkenness or idolatry. Galatians warns that those who practise such things will not inherit the kingdom of God.

Galatians 5:19–21 · 1 John 2:15–17

This list does not identify people to despise. It calls every one of us to examine ourselves before God. In 1 Corinthians 6:11, Paul also reminds believers that they were washed, sanctified and justified. Their past did not have the final word.

Romans 3:23–24 · 1 Corinthians 6:11

05 / Respond today

Repent.
Believe in Jesus.
Follow Him.

The Son of God gave His life for our sins. He suffered on the cross, and God raised Him from the dead. He calls us out of darkness and into His light.

1 Peter 2:24 · 1 Corinthians 15:3–4 · John 3:19–21

Acknowledge your sins. Change direction.

Come to God without making excuses. Confess your sins. Leave the practices that turn you away from Him, make amends where possible, and ask for His help to walk in obedience.

Acts 3:19 · Acts 26:20

Respond to the call to be baptized.

Speak with a disciple of Jesus or a church faithful to Scripture to understand the gospel and be baptized by immersion. Continue in faith, prayer, fellowship and obedience to Jesus.

Romans 6:3–4 · Acts 2:41–42 · Matthew 28:19–20
